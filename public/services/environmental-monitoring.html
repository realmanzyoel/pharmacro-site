Questions

Answers first.

What does Annex 1 require for Grade A monitoring?#

Continuous non-viable particle monitoring for the full duration of critical processing, and viable monitoring (active air, settle plates, gloves) throughout. Any growth in Grade A should be investigated.

What are the Grade A to D particle limits?#

Per Annex 1 Table 1 (≥0.5 µm per m³): Grade A 3,520 at rest and in operation; B 3,520 at rest and 352,000 in operation; C 352,000 at rest and 3,520,000 in operation; D 3,520,000 at rest.

How many locations do I need for ISO 14644-1 classification?#

It depends on room area, from ISO 14644-1:2015 Table A.1: for example 6 locations up to 24 m² and 10 up to 52 m². The builder on this page calculates it.

Alert versus action levels: what is the difference?#

Action levels come from Annex 1 limits; exceeding them needs investigation. Alert levels are set from your own historical data to warn of drift before you reach action levels.

Do you sample on site?#

Yes. Our field team gowns into your cleanrooms, follows your SOPs and hands samples back on a signed chain of custody form.

How quickly will I know about an excursion?#

Our target is a call within 24 hours of a read above the action level, with the isolate identified as soon as it grows.

Can you test purified water and WFI?#

Yes: TOC (USP <643>), conductivity (<645>), endotoxin (≤0.25 EU/mL for WFI) and microbial count on R2A.

Do you support media fills?#

We incubate and read media-fill units and identify any contaminant. Design and execution of the simulation remain your responsibility.

Guides

Short guides for QA and production teams.

Grade A to D requirements at a glance

Grade A is the critical zone: filling, open sterile product, stopper bowls. Grade B is its background for aseptic preparation. Grades C and D support less critical steps.

Annex 1 viable limits (Table 6): Grade A no growth; B 10 CFU/m³ air, 5 CFU/4 h settle, 5 CFU per contact plate and glove; C 100, 50 and 25; D 200, 100 and 50.

Excursion response playbook

Within an hour: confirm the read and photograph the plate. Same day: identify the organism and check the batch timeline. Within 72 hours: impact assessment on product made in that window.

Close with a CAPA only when the root cause is supported by data, not by habit.

Trending and alert/action limits

Set alert levels from 12 months of data, for example the 95th percentile per location. Review them yearly. Trend by location, organism and shift, not only by room.

Repeated Gram-positive cocci usually point at people. Spore-formers point at materials and cleaning.
