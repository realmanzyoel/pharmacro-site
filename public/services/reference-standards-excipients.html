Questions

Answers first.

Do I need full monograph testing on every lot?#

Not always. After supplier qualification, many companies use reduced testing (identity plus selected tests) with periodic full testing, justified by supplier data and audits. Regulators expect identity on every container for some materials.

Which pharmacopoeia should I test against?#

The one cited in your marketing authorisation. For global products we can test against several and report each result against each limit.

Why test glycerin for DEG and EG?#

Diethylene glycol contamination of glycerin and other excipients has caused deaths in several countries. USP requires a DEG and EG limit test in the Glycerin monograph identification, at 0.10% each.

Can you qualify a working reference standard?#

Yes: identity, purity, water, residue, assigned content with uncertainty against a primary standard, and a retest date.

What is pharmacopoeial harmonisation?#

The Pharmacopoeial Discussion Group (USP, Ph. Eur., JP, with IP joining in 2023) harmonises selected excipient monographs and general chapters so one test satisfies all three.

Can you test APIs too?#

Yes, against compendial monographs or your specification, including related substances, residual solvents and elemental impurities.

Do you do identity on every container?#

Yes, with handheld Raman through the bag or bottle where the method is validated for that material.

What if my supplier CoA and your result disagree?#

We investigate our result first, then share data so you can raise it with the supplier.

Guides

Why every ingredient deserves your attention.

Why supplier audits are not enough

An audit shows a system on the day you visit. Testing shows the material in your hand. Use both: audits set your confidence, testing confirms each lot, and trending catches drift.

Counterfeit and adulterated excipient risks

Glycerin, propylene glycol and sorbitol solutions have been adulterated with DEG and EG. Heparin was adulterated with oversulphated chondroitin sulphate in 2008. High-value or high-volume liquid excipients deserve specific tests, not only identity.

Pharmacopoeial harmonisation explained

Harmonised monographs and chapters (such as <61>, <62>, dissolution and many excipients) mean one test result can support filings in the US, EU and Japan. Check the harmonisation status in each pharmacopoeia, because local additions can remain.
