Questions

Answers first.

What does ICH M10 cover?#

Validation and study-sample analysis for chromatographic and ligand-binding assays measuring drugs and metabolites in biological matrices, including ISR, cross-validation and reporting.

What is a tiered immunogenicity approach?#

Samples are screened; screen-positives are confirmed by drug inhibition; confirmed positives are titred and tested for neutralising activity. Each tier uses a statistically derived cut point.

How is the screening cut point set?#

From drug-naive samples, usually 50 or more individuals, targeting a 5% false-positive rate so that true positives are not missed.

What is drug tolerance?#

The drug concentration at which the assay still detects ADA. Acid dissociation and other pre-treatments improve it.

Do you run incurred sample reanalysis?#

Yes: 10% of samples up to 1,000 and 5% beyond, as M10 requires. Two thirds must agree within 20% for chromatographic assays and 30% for ligand-binding assays.

LC-MS/MS or ligand binding for a biologic?#

Ligand binding is the default for most proteins; LC-MS/MS with signature peptides helps when reagents are poor or specificity is critical.

How long are samples stored?#

As your protocol and contract say, at −80 °C with alarmed monitoring. Long-term stability in matrix must cover the storage period.

Can you transfer a method from another lab?#

Yes, with partial validation or cross-validation as M10 describes.

Guides

Immunogenicity and bioanalysis, in plain words.

Immunogenicity risk assessment, explained

Start with the molecule (sequence, aggregates, impurities), the patient (immune status, disease) and the treatment (route, frequency, duration). Higher risk drives earlier and more frequent ADA sampling and a NAb assay that reflects the mechanism.

ICH M10 key points

One guideline for chromatographic and ligand-binding assays. Full validation covers selectivity, specificity, matrix effect, calibration, accuracy and precision, carry-over, dilution integrity and stability. Accuracy acceptance: ±15% (±20% at LLOQ) for chromatography; ±20% (±25% at LLOQ and ULOQ) for ligand binding.

Bioanalytical method validation checklist

Reference standard with CoA. Blank matrix from at least six sources. Stable-isotope internal standard where possible. Stability: bench-top, freeze-thaw, long-term, stock solution, processed sample. A written plan before the first run.
