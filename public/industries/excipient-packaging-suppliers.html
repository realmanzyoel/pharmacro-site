Regulatory context

What your regulators expect.

General information, not regulatory advice. Check the current text before acting.

Excipients. IPEC-PQG GMP guidance and EU guidelines on risk assessment for excipients shape what customers expect.

Packaging. USP <660> (glass), <661.1> and <661.2> (plastics) and <381> (elastomeric closures) define packaging tests; <1663> and <1664> cover E&L.

Contamination. USP and Ph. Eur. include DEG and EG limit tests for at-risk excipients such as glycerin and propylene glycol.

Harmonisation. The Pharmacopoeial Discussion Group (USP, Ph. Eur., JP, with IP joining in 2023) harmonises key monographs.

FAQ

Questions we hear from excipient and packaging suppliers.

Which pharmacopoeias do you test to?#

USP-NF, Ph. Eur., JP and IP, plus customer-specific specifications.

Do you test for DEG and EG?#

Yes, by GC per the current monographs.

Can you test packaging components?#

Yes: glass, plastics and elastomers to the relevant USP chapters, plus container closure integrity.

Do you run extractables studies?#

Yes, with controlled extraction and GC-MS, LC-MS and ICP-MS analysis.

Can your report go to our customers?#

Yes, through the portal or as a signed PDF.

Do you qualify reference standards?#

Yes, including in-house working standards against primary standards.
