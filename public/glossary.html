Glossary

128 pharmaceutical testing terms in plain words, from accelerated stability to Zone IVb.

The short answer

This glossary explains 128 terms used in pharmaceutical analytical testing, quality and regulation, in plain words. Use the A to Z bar or the search box. Every term has its own anchor, so you can link straight to it.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

A

Accelerated stability
Storage at elevated stress, usually 40 °C / 75% RH for six months, to predict long-term behaviour.
Acceptable intake (AI)
The daily intake of a mutagenic impurity, such as a nitrosamine, considered to pose negligible cancer risk over a lifetime.
Accuracy
How close a measured result is to the true value. A validation characteristic under ICH Q2(R2).
Action limit
An environmental monitoring level that, when exceeded, requires investigation and corrective action.
ADA
Anti-drug antibody: an antibody a patient makes against a biologic medicine. Measured in immunogenicity testing.
AET
Analytical evaluation threshold: the level above which an extractable or leachable must be identified and assessed.
ALCOA+
Data integrity principles: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available.
Alert limit
An environmental monitoring level that signals a possible drift from normal conditions before the action limit is reached.
API
Active pharmaceutical ingredient: the substance in a medicine that produces its effect.
Assay
A test that measures the amount of active ingredient, usually as a percentage of the label claim.
AUC (analytical ultracentrifugation)
A method that separates particles by sedimentation; the reference method for AAV empty/full capsid ratio.
Audit trail
A secure, computer-generated record of who created, changed or deleted data, when and why.

B

Batch release
The formal decision, by a qualified person or equivalent, that a batch meets its specification and may be supplied.
Bioburden
The number of viable microorganisms on or in a product or component before sterilisation.
Biosimilar
A biologic medicine highly similar to an approved reference biologic, with no clinically meaningful differences.
Bracketing
A stability design that tests only the extremes, such as highest and lowest strength, under ICH Q1D.

C

CAPA
Corrective and preventive action: the process for fixing a problem's cause and stopping it recurring.
Capping efficiency
The percentage of mRNA molecules with a correct 5' cap structure.
CCI
Container closure integrity: the ability of a package to keep out microorganisms and gases over shelf life.
CDSCO
Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation: India's national drug regulator.
CE-SDS
Capillary electrophoresis with sodium dodecyl sulfate: measures protein size variants and fragments.
CFU
Colony-forming unit: a count of viable microorganisms capable of forming a colony on a growth medium.
Change control
A formal process for proposing, assessing, approving and documenting changes in a GMP environment.
Chromatogram
The plot produced by chromatography showing detector response against time; peaks represent separated components.
CoA
Certificate of analysis: a signed document listing the tests performed on a batch and the results.
Comparability
Evidence that a product before and after a process change has comparable quality, safety and efficacy.
Contemporaneous
Recorded at the time the activity is performed. One of the ALCOA principles.
Content uniformity
A test that each dosage unit contains the active ingredient within a narrow range of the label claim.
CPCA
Carcinogenic potency categorisation approach: assigns NDSRIs to potency categories that set default acceptable intakes.
CQA
Critical quality attribute: a property that must stay within limits to ensure product quality.
CRO
Contract research organisation: a company that provides research or testing services under contract.
CTD Module 3
The quality section of the Common Technical Document used in regulatory submissions.

D

Data integrity
The completeness, consistency and accuracy of data throughout its life cycle.
ddPCR
Droplet digital PCR: absolute quantification of nucleic acids by partitioning a sample into thousands of droplets.
Degradant
An impurity formed when the drug substance or product breaks down over time.
Deviation
A departure from an approved procedure or specification that must be documented and assessed.
Dissolution
A test of how fast and how completely the active ingredient dissolves from a solid dosage form.
DLS
Dynamic light scattering: measures particle size from fluctuations in scattered light.
dsRNA
Double-stranded RNA: an impurity in mRNA manufacture that can trigger innate immune responses.
Dwell volume
The volume between the HPLC mixing point and the column head; differences shift gradient retention times.

E

E&L
Extractables and leachables: compounds that can migrate, or do migrate, from packaging or equipment into a product.
Electronic signature
A computer data compilation executed, adopted or authorised by a person as the legal equivalent of a handwritten signature.
Encapsulation efficiency
The proportion of mRNA inside lipid nanoparticles rather than free in solution.
Endotoxin
Lipopolysaccharide from Gram-negative bacteria that causes fever when injected.
Environmental monitoring (EM)
Routine sampling of air, surfaces and personnel in cleanrooms to show control of contamination.
EU (endotoxin unit)
The unit used to express endotoxin activity.
Excipient
Any ingredient of a medicine other than the active ingredient.
Extractables
Compounds released from a material under exaggerated laboratory conditions.

F

f2 similarity factor
A statistic that compares two dissolution profiles; 50 or above suggests similarity.
Forced degradation
Deliberate stress of a drug under heat, light, acid, base and oxidation to identify degradants and prove a method is stability-indicating.
FTIR
Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy: identifies materials by their infrared absorption.

G

GC-HS
Headspace gas chromatography: used to measure residual solvents.
GLP
Good Laboratory Practice: the quality system for non-clinical safety studies.
Glycan profiling
Characterisation of the sugar structures attached to a glycoprotein.
GMP
Good Manufacturing Practice: the quality system governing manufacture and testing of medicines.

H

HCP
Host cell proteins: process impurities from the cells used to make a biologic.
HPLC
High-performance liquid chromatography: separates compounds in a liquid by passing them through a packed column.

I

ICH
International Council for Harmonisation: develops harmonised guidelines for medicine quality, safety and efficacy.
ICH M10
The ICH guideline on bioanalytical method validation, adopted in 2022.
ICH Q14
The ICH guideline on analytical procedure development, adopted in 2023.
ICH Q1A(R2)
The ICH guideline on stability testing of new drug substances and products.
ICH Q2(R2)
The ICH guideline on validation of analytical procedures, revised and adopted in 2023.
ICH Q3D
The ICH guideline on elemental impurities, with permitted daily exposures for 24 elements.
icIEF
Imaged capillary isoelectric focusing: separates protein charge variants.
ICP-MS
Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry: measures elements at trace levels.
Identification test
A test that confirms a substance is what it is labelled to be.
Immunogenicity
The tendency of a medicine to provoke an immune response.
In-use stability
A study of how long a product stays within specification after the container is opened or the product is prepared.
Intermediate precision
Variation within one laboratory across days, analysts or equipment.
ISO 14644
The international standard series for cleanroom classification and monitoring.
ISO/IEC 17025
The international standard for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories.

K

Karl Fischer
A titration method for measuring water content.

L

LAL
Limulus amebocyte lysate: a reagent from horseshoe crab blood used to detect endotoxin.
LC-MS/MS
Liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry: sensitive and selective quantification of compounds.
Leachables
Compounds that migrate into a product from its packaging or contact materials under normal conditions.
Limit of detection (LOD)
The lowest amount of analyte that can be detected but not necessarily quantified.
Limit of quantitation (LOQ)
The lowest amount of analyte that can be measured with acceptable accuracy and precision.
LIMS
Laboratory information management system: software that manages samples, tests, results and reports.
Linearity
The ability of a method to give results proportional to analyte concentration over a range.
LNP
Lipid nanoparticle: the delivery vehicle for most mRNA medicines.
Long-term stability
Storage under the recommended condition for the climatic zone, over the proposed shelf life.

M

Matrixing
A stability design that tests a subset of samples at each time point, under ICH Q1D.
Method transfer
The documented process that qualifies a receiving laboratory to use an analytical method.
Method validation
Documented evidence that an analytical method is suitable for its intended purpose.
Method verification
Confirmation that a compendial method works in a given laboratory for a given product.
MSA
Master service agreement: a framework contract setting terms for future work orders.
MVD
Maximum valid dilution: the greatest dilution of a sample at which the endotoxin limit can still be detected.
Mycoplasma
Small bacteria without a cell wall that can contaminate cell cultures and biologic processes.

N

NAT
Nucleic acid amplification technique, such as PCR, used for rapid detection of organisms like mycoplasma.
NDSRI
Nitrosamine drug substance-related impurity: a nitrosamine that shares structure with the API.
Nitrosamine
A compound with an N-nitroso group; many are probable human carcinogens.

O

OOS
Out of specification: a result outside the established acceptance criteria.
OOT
Out of trend: a result within specification but unexpected compared with earlier data.
Orthogonal method
A method based on a different principle, used to confirm a result.

P

Part 11
US regulation 21 CFR Part 11 on electronic records and electronic signatures.
PDI
Polydispersity index: a measure of how broad a particle size distribution is.
Peptide mapping
Digestion of a protein into peptides and analysis by LC-MS to confirm its sequence and modifications.
Pharmacopoeia
An official book of standards for medicines, such as USP, Ph. Eur., JP and IP.
Photostability
Testing of how light affects a drug, under ICH Q1B.
PK
Pharmacokinetics: how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolises and eliminates a drug.
Poly(A) tail
The adenosine sequence at the 3' end of mRNA that supports stability and translation.
Polymorph
One of several crystal forms of the same compound, which may differ in solubility and stability.
Potency
A measure of biological activity, usually relative to a reference standard.
Precision
How close repeated measurements are to each other.
Pull point
A scheduled time at which stability samples are removed from storage and tested.

Q

QbD
Quality by design: a systematic approach that builds quality in from product and process understanding.
Qualified person (QP)
In the EU, the person legally responsible for certifying each batch before release.

R

Reference standard
A highly characterised material used as the benchmark in a test.
Related substances
Impurities structurally related to the API, such as by-products and degradants.
Residual solvents
Organic volatile chemicals left over from manufacture, limited under ICH Q3C.
rFC
Recombinant factor C: an animal-free reagent for endotoxin testing.
Robustness
A method's ability to stay unaffected by small, deliberate changes in conditions.

S

SEC
Size-exclusion chromatography: separates molecules by size, used for protein aggregates.
Specification
The list of tests, methods and acceptance criteria a product must meet.
Specificity
A method's ability to measure the analyte without interference from other components.
Stability-indicating method
A method that can detect changes in quality attributes over time, separating the API from its degradants.
Sterility test
A compendial test for viable microorganisms in a product labelled sterile, usually 14 days of incubation.
System suitability
Checks run with each analysis to confirm the system performs adequately on the day.

T

TAT
Turnaround time: time from sample receipt to issue of the report.
TOC
Total organic carbon: a test of organic contamination in pharmaceutical water.
True copy
A verified copy of an original record that preserves its content and meaning.

U

UPLC
Ultra-performance liquid chromatography: HPLC with small particles and high pressure for faster separations.
USP
United States Pharmacopeia: sets public standards for medicines in the US.

V

Validation protocol
A pre-approved plan that states how a validation will be done and what results are acceptable.
Vector genome titre
The number of vector genomes per millilitre in a gene therapy product.

W

WFI
Water for injections: highly purified water meeting pharmacopoeial limits for endotoxin and conductivity.

X

XRPD
X-ray powder diffraction: identifies crystal forms by their diffraction pattern.

Z

Zone IVb
The hot and very humid ICH climatic zone; long-term stability at 30 °C / 75% RH.
